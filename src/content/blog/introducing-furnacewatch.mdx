## The problem we set out to solve

When a gas furnace quits, the first person to know is usually the homeowner — and only
because the house got cold. The thermostat still reads "heating." Nothing looks wrong.
By the time the call reaches a service company, it's an emergency: an unhappy customer,
a truck rolling without a diagnosis, and a technician starting from scratch in the driveway.

The frustrating part is that the furnace was telling the whole story all along — the heat
call that went unanswered, the inducer that never spun up, the burner that lit and dropped
out. Nobody was listening.

## What we built

FurnaceWatch is a sensor that a service technician mounts on the furnace during a normal
maintenance visit. It powers from the furnace's own 24V transformer, senses the heat call
from the thermostat wires, and follows every heating cycle — inducer, ignition, blower —
around the clock.

When a cycle goes wrong, the failure gets a name: heat call with no ignition, igniter
failure, flame sensor failure, a pressure switch or rollout trip, short cycling. Your
office gets a push notification with the failure mode and the timeline, and the portal
shows every furnace under agreement in one list.

Detection runs on the sensor itself, so a Wi-Fi drop at the home never means an
unwatched furnace.

## Where we are

We've been building quietly for a while: the hardware, the on-device detection —
validated on instrumented furnaces, with wider validation underway — and the fleet
portal your office and your customers log into.

This heating season we're enrolling a small number of HVAC service companies as design
partners: real maintenance-agreement fleets, honest feedback, and a direct line to the
person building the product.
